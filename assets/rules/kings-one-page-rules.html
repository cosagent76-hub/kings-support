Kings One-Page Rules

2-6 players | 13 hands | lowest score wins
Goal Go out first to score 0. Everyone else counts cards left in hand. Lowest total after hand 13 wins.
On Your Turn Draw 1, play cards if you can, then discard 1. If your last card is played, the hand ends.
Use The App Kings keeps score, tracks the wild rank, and saves game history. The cards are still played at the table.

1. Setup

  • Use one standard deck plus two jokers.
  • Deal 7 cards to each player. The dealer gets 8 cards and takes the first turn.
  • The dealer does not draw first. The dealer discards 1 card to start the discard pile.

2. Make Melds

  • Set: 3 or 4 cards with the same rank. Suits do not matter. A set cannot have more than 4 cards total.
  • Run: 3 or more cards in order, all the same suit. Aces are low, so A-2-3 works; Q-K-A does not.
  • You can lay down your own meld, then add to any player's meld on that same turn.

3. Wild Cards

  • Jokers are always wild.
  • The wild rank changes by hand: hand 1 Aces, hand 2 Twos, and so on through hand 13 Kings.
  • In a set, the meld must have more real cards than wilds. Lay the wild sideways across the bottom.
  • In a run, place the wild upright where the missing card belongs. Wilds cannot sit next to each other.

4. Opening, Adding, Going Out

  • Before adding to another player's meld, lay down your own set or run first. This is called opening.
  • If you have the real card a wild represents in a run, you may replace it and take the wild, or cover it and leave the wild underneath.
  • If you can go out, you must go out. You do not need a final discard.

5. Scoring

  • Aces count 1. Cards 2-10 count their number. Jacks, Queens, and Kings count 10.
  • Jokers and the current wild rank count 20 if left in your hand.
  • Optional table rules are fine. Agree on them before hand 1 starts.

Table Reminders

  • Keep your hand private around the table.
  • Keep food and drinks away from the draw pile, discard pile, and melds.
  • Learning game: keep the help page open for examples.
  • Short game: stop after an agreed hand and lowest score wins.
